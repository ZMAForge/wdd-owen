Peanut Butter Summary

Peanut Butter is a story about a capitalist society, and the harmful effects it can unknowingly, or knowingly have on society, nature, and our planet. The story follows a man named Asher Pezi who has a severe peanut allergy. It follows him through getting fired from his job, and the things he's willing to do for money to stay on his feet.

As Asher spirals deeper into the gigs economy's unforgiving maw, the narrative shifts its lens toword the corporate machinery that prioritizes profit margins over human biology. The "Peanut" becomes a stinging metaphor for the pervasive, inescapable reach of industrial output- a substance that is life-sustaining for many, yet literal poison for Asher. His struggle to find a safe haven highlights a grim irony: in a world driven by mass production and unregulated expansion, the environment itself becomes hostile to those who dont "fit" the standard mold. Through his increasingly desperate attempts to secure a paycheck, the story exposes how capitalism doesn't just exploit labor; it forces individuals to gamble with their very existence.
